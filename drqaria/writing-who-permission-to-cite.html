I asked WHO for permission to cite its data. It answered.

When I first wrote, I did not expect a reply. On 29 October 2026 the final answer came. Here is what I asked, what WHO asked me back, and what the permission does not mean.

Published 7 November 2026 2 min read Dr. Qaria

A scientist running a small company writes to the World Health Organization and asks to use its data. When I first wrote, I did not expect an answer. On 29 October 2026 the WHO Permissions Team replied that the use I had described may proceed, and can be handled as citation of selected data.

What did WHO allow PharmaTrust to do?

WHO's Permissions Team said PharmaTrust may cite selected numerical specification data, with attribution. The source is The International Pharmacopoeia. That is permission to cite data. WHO does not endorse PharmaTrust, NJMC or me, is not affiliated with us, and its logo may not be used.

Why I asked

I have repeatedly seen buyers accept raw materials and finished medicines on documents they had no means of checking. Many do not hold a copy of the standard the certificate names. The International Pharmacopoeia concentrates on medicines that matter in low and middle income countries. That is why I asked for this book.

My first request was too broad

I asked for numerical limits across the essential medicines. In September WHO asked me which content I meant, how much of it, how it would be shown, and whether users would be sent back to the source. Those questions were fair, and they made me think properly about what the tool needs.

I narrowed the request to 30 monographs and numbers only: test name, method reference, limit and unit. No monograph text is held, and nobody can browse, search or download the limits. They appear only for the tests on the customer's own certificate.

What changes in the report

The limits are used only when The International Pharmacopoeia is the sole standard a certificate claims, and the product matches one of the 30 monographs by name. The report then shows the reported result beside the cited limit, names the monograph and edition, and states that The International Pharmacopoeia remains the authoritative standard and that the analysis does not replace it.

A qualified person still makes the decision, and the report may not catch every problem.

What the permission does not mean

It is permission to cite data. WHO does not endorse PharmaTrust, NJMC or me, and is not affiliated with us. The WHO logo is not used anywhere in this work. I am writing this because the answer mattered to me and because the exact wording matters, so I would rather state the limits myself.

Source: The International Pharmacopoeia, Thirteenth Edition, 2025, World Health Organization.
